% ---------------------------------------- RESULTS, psychometric paragraph --
% Keep the fit description and the ANOVA/Mauchly sentences as you wrote them.
% DELETE: "One participant-by-finger fit (natural setup, middle finger) had a PSE outside the
%          tested range ... yielded a significant Finger effect on JND (...)."  (that participant
%          is now excluded by the success criterion, so there is no sensitivity ANOVA)
% APPEND after "...the correction factor $\varepsilon$ is reported with each effect.":
Because a non-significant ANOVA cannot establish the absence of an effect, we also ran two one-sided equivalence tests (TOST)~\cite{lakens2017} on the same per-participant fits. Bounds were pre-specified in perceptual units: for PSE bias, $\pm$1~JND ($\pm$1.6~mm/m; median per-participant JND 1.61~mm/m), because two conditions whose PSEs differ by less than one JND are indistinguishable to the participants themselves, with $\pm$0.5~JND ($\pm$0.8~mm/m) as a stricter secondary bound; for JND, $\pm$0.5~mm/m (about 30\% of the JND). Setup comparisons (20 vs.\ 19 participants) used Welch's $t$ with Welch--Satterthwaite degrees of freedom, which requires neither equal group sizes nor equal variances~\cite{delacre2017}; finger pairs used paired TOSTs with Holm correction, and the Finger factor was called equivalent only if all six pairs passed. Equivalence was declared when the 90\% confidence interval lay within the bounds. Default JZS Bayes factors~\cite{rouder2009} and the minimal detectable effect at 80\% power are reported alongside.

% ------------------------------------------- RESULTS, after the ANOVA table --
% FIND: "Neither PSE bias nor JND showed significant effects of Setup or Finger combination (Table~\ref{tab:anova}). The psychometric fit to all trials pooled ... Table~\ref{tab:psychometric-fit}."
% REPLACE WITH:
PSE bias showed no significant effect of Setup, Finger, or their interaction (Table~\ref{tab:anova}). For JND, the Setup effect was not significant, but the Finger effect was ($p_{\mathrm{GG}}=.042$, $\eta_p^2=.075$): Holm-corrected pairwise comparisons showed a higher JND for the middle than the ring finger (medians 2.33 vs.\ 1.53~mm/m; $t(38)=3.63$, $p=.005$, $d=0.55$), with no other pair differing (all $p\geq.60$). The psychometric fit to all trials pooled across fingers and setups gave a PSE of $8.41$~mm/m (95\% CI $[8.22, 8.59]$), i.e.\ a bias of $-0.09$~mm/m ($[-0.28, 0.09]$, $p_{\mathrm{bias}=0}=.33$), and a JND of $2.47$~mm/m ($[2.29, 2.64]$; Weber fraction $0.29$). Finger-level pooled and per-participant estimates are given in Table~\ref{tab:psychometric-fit}.

Equivalence tests (Fig.~\ref{fig:equivalence}) supported the null result for bias. The setup difference in bias was equivalent within $\pm$1~JND (air-slide minus natural $=0.26$~mm/m, 90\% CI $[-0.10, 0.62]$, $t(34.9)=-6.32$, $p_{\mathrm{TOST}}<.001$) and within $\pm$0.5~JND ($p_{\mathrm{TOST}}=.008$); each finger's bias was equivalent to zero within $\pm$0.5~JND (all $p_{\mathrm{TOST}}\leq.013$), and all six finger-pair differences were equivalent within $\pm$1~JND (Holm-corrected $p_{\mathrm{TOST}}\leq.001$). Bayes factors favoured the null only weakly ($\mathrm{BF}_{01}=1.8$ for setup; 2.3--5.4 for the finger pairs). For JND, equivalence could not be established: JND was $0.92$~mm/m higher in the air-slide setup (90\% CI $[0.09, 1.75]$, $p_{\mathrm{TOST}}=.80$; Welch $t(33.0)=1.89$, $p=.068$, $\mathrm{BF}_{01}=0.8$) and higher for the middle than the ring finger ($1.19$~mm/m, $[0.64, 1.74]$, $\mathrm{BF}_{10}=36$); the other pairs neither differed nor reached equivalence. With 20 vs.\ 19 participants and the observed variability, the design had 80\% power to detect a setup difference of $0.62$~mm/m in bias and $1.42$~mm/m in JND.

% ------------------------------------------------------------ DISCUSSION ----
% FIND: "Although success was slightly higher in the natural than air-slide setup (81.3\% vs. 78.5\%),
%        neither Setup, Finger, nor their interaction significantly affected (bias) or JND."
% REPLACE WITH:
Although success was slightly higher in the natural than the air-slide setup (82.8\% vs.\ 78.5\%), PSE bias was unaffected by setup or finger, and equivalence tests bounded any bias difference within one JND. Because the standard and comparison were rendered on the same finger, a non-zero bias would indicate a rendering artefact rather than a perceptual property; its equivalence to zero on every finger therefore validates the display in both setups. Sensitivity was less uniform: JND did not differ significantly between setups but could not be shown equivalent, and the middle finger was less sensitive than the ring finger by about 1.2~mm/m, half a JND.
% FIND: "The absence of a systematic finger effect may be useful for teleoperation system design,"
% REPLACE WITH:
The absence of a finger effect on bias, and the small size of the only sensitivity difference, may be useful for teleoperation system design,
% APPEND to the limitations paragraph (after "...backlash, and hysteresis."):
A second limitation is statistical: with 39 participants, equivalence could be established for bias but not for JND, whose between-participant variability was larger; a $\pm$0.5~mm/m bound on JND would require several hundred participants per setup.
