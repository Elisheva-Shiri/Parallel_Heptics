% ===== METHODS: append to the psychometric-analysis paragraph, after the
% ===== Greenhouse--Geisser sentence.
Because a non-significant ANOVA cannot establish the absence of an effect, we also tested equivalence with two one-sided tests (TOST)~\cite{lakens2017} on the same per-participant fits. Bounds were pre-specified in perceptual units: for PSE bias, $\pm$1~JND ($\pm$1.6~mm/m; the median per-participant JND was 1.61~mm/m), because two conditions whose PSEs differ by less than one JND are indistinguishable to the participants themselves, with $\pm$0.5~JND ($\pm$0.8~mm/m) as a stricter secondary bound; for JND, $\pm$0.5~mm/m (about 30\% of the JND) and, because JND is a scale parameter, a $\times$1.30 ratio bound on the log scale. Setup comparisons (20 vs.\ 19 participants) used Welch's $t$ with Welch--Satterthwaite degrees of freedom, which requires neither equal group sizes nor equal variances~\cite{delacre2017,welch1947}; finger pairs used paired TOSTs with Holm correction, and the Finger factor was called equivalent only if all six pairs passed. Equivalence was declared when the 90\% confidence interval lay within the bounds, equivalently $\max(p_{\mathrm{lower}},p_{\mathrm{upper}})<.05$. Default JZS Bayes factors~\cite{rouder2009} ($r=0.707$; robustness over $r=0.35$--$1.0$) and the minimal detectable effect at 80\% power are reported alongside.

% ===== RESULTS PARAGRAPH (after the mixed-design ANOVA results)
Equivalence tests (Table~\ref{tab:tost}) supported the null result for PSE bias. The setup difference in bias was statistically equivalent within $\pm$1~JND (air-slide minus natural $=0.25$~mm/m, 90\% CI $[-0.10, 0.61]$, $t(34.9)=-6.17$, $p_{\mathrm{TOST}}<.001$) and within the stricter $\pm$0.5~JND bound ($p_{\mathrm{TOST}}=.007$), and also within $\pm$1~JND for each finger separately (all $p_{\mathrm{TOST}}\le.013$). All six finger-pair differences in bias were equivalent within $\pm$1~JND after Holm correction (largest $p_{\mathrm{TOST}}=.001$), and each finger's bias was equivalent to zero within $\pm$0.5~JND (all $p_{\mathrm{TOST}}\le.012$). Bayes factors pointed the same way but only weakly: BF$_{01}=1.8$ for the setup difference (1.3--2.3 across prior widths) and 2.4--5.4 for the finger pairs. For JND, equivalence could not be established at either bound: JND was $0.92$~mm/m higher in the air-slide setup (90\% CI $[0.09, 1.75]$; ratio 1.51, 90\% CI $[1.01, 2.25]$; Welch $t(33.0)=1.89$, $p=.068$; BF$_{01}=0.8$), and the middle finger's JND exceeded the ring finger's ($1.19$~mm/m, 90\% CI $[0.64, 1.74]$, $p=.001$, BF$_{10}=36$ on the raw scale, $1.1$ on the log scale), whereas the remaining five pairs neither differed nor reached equivalence. With 20 vs.\ 19 participants and the observed variability, the design had 80\% power to detect a setup difference of $0.62$~mm/m in bias and $1.42$~mm/m in JND.

% ===== DISCUSSION (one calibrated sentence; replace any "did not differ" claim)
The setup and finger null effects on PSE bias are therefore supported by evidence for equivalence within one JND, whereas for JND the data are inconclusive at the perceptual bound and lean toward a modestly higher JND in the air-slide setup and for the middle finger; the null on sensitivity should not be read as evidence of no difference.
